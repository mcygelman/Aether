[ FACE_REFERENCE_AND_BLINK ]
When the operator selects a face mockup, preserve that choice instead of substituting a different
head direction. In the 2026-11-04 approved mockup, a compact eye pair toward the right-facing muzzle
worked better than moving the muzzle. Keep complete eye shapes, pupils and glints together, and
apply the same per-eye offsets to the corresponding closed lids. A correct open pose does not
certify the blink. Inspect open, transition and closed frames under the unchanged blink clock.
Offsets and overlap are reference-specific, not universal anatomy rules. A text-only worker should
attribute a supplied visual interpretation and must not claim it viewed a PNG.

[ ALGORITHM ]
1. Establish a silhouette and coordinate table: torso bounds, head center/radius or outline, facing
   direction, hip/shoulder joints, knee/ankle joints if used, paw shapes and tail/ear anchors.
   Rear/front names describe anatomy; far/near names describe depth. They are not interchangeable.
   Place rear joints in the torso's rear region and front joints near the chest; do not copy absolute
   coordinates or one ellipse's proportions as anatomy rules for every style.

2. Derive facial features from the head geometry. For a circular head, candidate crown points can
   use x=cx+r*cos(theta), y=cy-r*sin(theta), with theta measured counterclockwise from right in this
   formula. Inset ear base points into the silhouette for robust overlap. This is a useful sufficient
   construction, not the only connected triangle. Check the actual ellipse/path, not only its box.
   Place eyes relative to the face direction and intended perspective. No universal far-eye scale
   ratio or requirement that both eyes lie in one half of the head. Check at intended display size.

3. Choose depth deliberately. A useful side-view order is far limbs/tail, torso, near limbs, head
   details, with exceptions for the desired pose. An upper far leg can be covered while its lower
   leg remains readable. Drawing every leg after the body is one stylized solution, not a law.
   Check ear bases, tail root, neck and all hips both at rest and at motion extremes.

4. Parent each joint to its support. Default construction:
   <g transform="translate(hipX hipY)">
     <g><animateTransform attributeName="transform" type="rotate"
       values="-15;15;-15" dur="0.9s" repeatCount="indefinite"/>
       <!-- leg drawn about local joint 0,0 -->
     </g>
   </g>
   Knees can repeat this structure. Use one named anchor per articulated part. Explicit
   animateTransform rotate values with cx/cy, or additive="sum" with understood transform order,
   are valid alternatives. CSS transform-origin is not forbidden; set its reference box and pivot
   deliberately. Never accidentally replace the placement transform or double-apply world offsets.

5. Define the gait before decorative bob: identify each of four legs, its phase offset, stance
   interval and swing interval. A hinge or hip/knee rig can work; complexity is not proof of quality.
   Alternating angles or four different begin times alone do not establish a particular biological
   gait. Name the intended stylized walk and inspect its footfall order.

6. Solve contact from the transformed paw boundary. For an unrotated, unscaled, filled paw ellipse,
   local lowest y = hip_y + paw_cy + paw_ry. Include other ancestors, bob, rotation and stroke when
   applicable. Translation_y = support_y - local_lowest_y only holds for that simplified pose.
   A center coordinate is not the contact boundary. In 3D-looking art, state a visible top-surface
   depth band; do not silently equate it to the rail's front face.

   During stance, the foot velocity relative to the support should be near zero. In screen
   coordinates: v_root + v_foot_relative_to_root ~= v_support. During swing, the foot lifts and
   advances. Do not force every foot to touch at every frame. Coordinate bob and joint motion so
   all feet do not float or sink together. Choose and record a style-appropriate tolerance.

7. Sample one full stride, both extreme poses, stance transitions and the repeat boundary.
   Inspect joints and paws at enlarged scale, then the whole cat at normal display size. A readable
   face, color contrast and a coherent silhouette matter as well as source correctness. Add blinks,
   ear flicks, tail motion and small bob only after the primary walk is readable.

[ EXAMPLES ]
[Setup]: Paw center y=21, radius=3, hip y=5; the intended support line is y=280.
[Process]: In the unrotated zero-bob pose, the local paw bottom is 29, so root translation is 251.
[Result]: Using 257 instead places the bottom at 286. This calculation illustrates Guide A's missing
radius/ancestor terms; it is not a repair instruction for every phase of its supplied animation.

[ SELF_CHECK ]
Q1: A head and ear share a group but have a visible gap. What changes?
A1: The drawn geometry; parenting alone cannot fix that gap.
Q2: Are dark far legs behind the body automatically wrong?
A2: No. Inspect their attachment and readable portions over the gait.
Q3: Does sinusoidal bob prove walking?
A3: No. Verify distinct limb phases, footfalls and support contact.
Q4: The kinematic calculation passes, but the actual cat.svg fails XML parsing. Is it delivered?
A4: No. Correct the file in your active task and rerun that failed parser check with the host's
required retry protocol. Report visual quality unverified until actual rendering is inspected.
