[ TITLE ]
Design an SVG Background and Its Support Surface

[ PURPOSE ]
Build readable SVG scenery with deliberate depth, complete support and restrained secondary motion.

STATUS: PILOT MODULE. Background-only work is a complete task. Do not insert a cat, a walk or a
fence unless requested. This module owns scenery geometry and visual treatment; scrolling uses
SVG_BACKGROUND_SCROLL, and figure travel uses SVG_FIGURE_CROSSING.

[ ALGORITHM ]
1. Extract the requested setting, mood, palette, display size and focal subject. If another agent
   will provide a figure, agree on viewBox, facing direction, support height/depth band, depth order,
   reserved silhouette area and motion ownership. Supply these as a small interface note.

2. Plan layers: sky/light, distant land, near scenery, support, optional figure slot, foreground.
   This is a starting layout, not a universal paint order. A near post or grass tuft can occlude a
   figure intentionally; document that choice so a reviewer can distinguish it from a missing part.

3. Build the requested support before decorative detail. For a fence, make rails and posts coherent
   where the design requires them, continue the surface under the visible travel route, and inspect
   both edges. A fence can legitimately have a different rail count or style than the examples.
   For a grass scene, the ground is the support; importing a fence is a transfer error.

4. Use repeated geometry deliberately. Specify post pitch and decorative repeat; align the last
   interval as well as the first. Use defs/use, gradients and clipping where they simplify work.
   Parse and check their references. Define the full repeat unit before promising a seamless scroll.

5. Use contrast and scale to keep the focal area readable. Check a small preview: the silhouette
   should remain clear against scenery. If the background itself is the requested focus, spend
   detail there without adding a character to justify it.

6. Add independent secondary motion where it supports the scene: clouds, light, foliage or distant
   creatures. Vary phase and period deliberately. Finite decimal periods are rational and can share
   a long common repeat; do not claim they are mathematically incommensurate. Keep background motion
   evidence separate from evidence about a character's gait.

7. Deliver the background alone and its interface note if that is the task. Inspect static quality,
   clipping and seams at target size, then any animation extrema. Document the target embed and a
   reduced-motion/static treatment when required. No need to load the character lesson for this work.

[ EXAMPLES ]
[Setup]: The task asks for a sunset fence background that another agent will place a cat upon.
[Process]: Supply the complete fence and scenery, plus its support band and layer/motion contract.
[Result]: The background is independently reviewable; character geometry and gait stay in their own module.

[ SELF_CHECK ]
Q1: Should a fox-on-grass request inherit the fence from these examples?
A1: No. Preserve the new brief's support and setting.
Q2: Is identical post spacing enough for a seamless wooden fence?
A2: Only if all visible details repeat at that spacing; otherwise use the full visual tile.
